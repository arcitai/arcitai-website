## Start

### Project inquiry.

Describe the software, who uses it and the work you need help with. I’ll review the context and respond with a proposed next step.

### Do I need another platform?

Often we can work with the tools you already use, with clearer instructions, checks and review. I add new tools only when they solve a real need for your project.

### What do you check?

We agree what production-ready means for your use case, then review the code, data access, integrations and deployment. I fix relevant findings and check the changes again. Instructions alone don’t enforce permissions, and a review isn’t a guarantee that nothing can go wrong.

### Can you keep looking after it?

Yes. I can take care of agreed updates, fixes and ongoing development. We define the systems I’m responsible for, the review process and the support you need, including response times. You stay involved in business decisions and approvals.

## Editorial decisions

- Use descriptive headings, familiar navigation and direct actions. Avoid sales prompts such as “Show me what you’ve got”, “Talk it through” and “Let’s take it further”. Preserve the calm first-person voice in explanatory prose. Restore the existing Offers selector, rather than replacing it with a renamed section link.
- The latest Software & Defence Factory decisions are background context, not public copy. Describe the consequence for the customer: scoped implementation, tested changes, review and agreed ongoing work. Do not name the factory, its runtime, skills, dashboard or release maturity on the landing page.
- onlinesourdough supports the customer's own AI practice through methods, resources and guidance. Arc’IT takes care of agreed technical delivery and ongoing work. Do not imply a mandatory AIOS or factory purchase.
- Remove the optional dashboard/example link from the landing page. Preserve the old concept files as history; do not relabel them as the implemented factory.
- “Production-ready” is the intended outcome for the agreed use case, not an unconditional claim of security or readiness before testing. Keep promising demo, MVP and prototype as recognizable starting points rather than a new branded offer name.
- A working prototype is a useful starting point. Do not imply that every AI-built tool is unsafe or that Arc’IT can remove all technical debt.
- Keep the value practical: inspect, prioritise, fix, test and establish responsibility. No invented customer results, prices, certifications or vendor relationship.
